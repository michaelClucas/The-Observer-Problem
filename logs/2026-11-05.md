### What I concluded

**Read 1: P-1 moves `PLAUSIBLE` -> `CONFIRMED`.** The trinity
equivalence's hypothesis, §V opening paragraph verbatim, is that "the
clock Hamiltonian `\hat H_C` has a continuous non-degenerate spectrum".
Boundedness is not a hypothesis; it is raised at source line 1258 inside
§V and explicitly discharged ("this is of no concern to us"), and two of
the three admissible clock examples in §III.2.3 have bounded spectra.
P-1 named exactly this as its settlement condition. Run 01 relation 7's
trailing inference — "so the compatibility claim and the spectrum question
are not independent" — **does not follow and is withdrawn**. Run 01 is
append-only and was not edited.

**Read 2: the answer is no, and in the authors' own words.** §3.6 verbatim:
"since the observer was put into the spacetime 'by hand' we need to bound
its physical properties [...] it is physically reasonable to bound the
energy `eps` of the observer from below", and the projector `P_{q>0}`
stands in the physical algebra in both §3.6 and §5. The energy bound is
**imposed exactly as in CLPW**, so WP-7's conditional has a false
antecedent and the de Sitter entropy normalisation stays a modelling
choice.

What the paper does establish is sharper than Run 01 had: Type II_1 is
**not robust** to the full constraint set. The infinite family of boost
supertranslation charges would be free zero modes giving
Type II_infinity, and what suppresses them is the observer's rotational
kinetic energy and **non-gravitational binding energy** — so the existence
of a maximum-entropy state for de Sitter is contingent on the stiffness of
a laboratory-scale body's internal forces. The claim's strength is
"argue" and "appears likely" throughout, never "prove".

**Read 3: the trade-off is stated in print.** De Vuyst et al. give the
dichotomy directly — ideal clock means spectrum `R`, Type II_infinity, no
maximum entropy state; bounded below means Type II_1 with a finite trace
and one — and add that CLPW's imposed lower bound is *equivalent* to
starting from non-ideal clocks with non-trivial projectors. They set the
two ends against each other explicitly (Alice ideal, Bob bounded, different
entropies). **Run 01's open question 3 is answered: yes, published, at
arXiv:2405.00114.** Run 01's "no retrieved source states the trade-off in
these terms" was a retrieval gap, not a gap in the literature.

### The numbers, and the one I will not use

`verify/clock_trace_finiteness.py` checks the convergence content only. It
establishes no physics of this repository's own, and says so in its
docstring.

- KKS's first trace factor `int_0^inf dq e^{-q}` evaluates to 1 exactly.
- The isotropic-body rotational sum converges for `I` in 0.1 to 1000.
- `Z_lm = sqrt(pi/c_lm)`, agreeing with quadrature to 1e-6, finite for
  every `c_lm > 0` and divergent as `c_lm -> 0` — i.e. the limit that
  destroys the finite trace is exactly the free-zero-mode limit the paper
  names. `[INFERENCE]`, premise named in the script: the group algebra
  carries Lebesgue measure.
- `||t||^2 = measure(sigma_c)`, so clock states are normalisable only for
  a doubly bounded spectrum.

**Reading limitation, recorded rather than smoothed over.** KKS's improper
basis `|e_SO(3)> = sum sqrt(2l+1) delta_{mn}|l,n,m>` appears to me to give
multiplicity `(2l+1)^2`, not the `(2l+1)` the paper writes, after summing
the `(2l+1)` terms with `n = m`. I could not reproduce the single factor
from the text alone and **I am not asserting the paper is wrong.** The
script evaluates both; both converge at every `I` tested, so the Type II_1
conclusion does not hinge on it. The *numerical* entropy contribution does:
`log tau` is 0.863032 under the paper's form and 1.737055 under the
variant, at `I = 1`. **That number is `[UNKNOWN]` and nothing uses it.**
