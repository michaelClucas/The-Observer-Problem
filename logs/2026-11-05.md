### What I concluded

**The structural fact that makes the job well-posed, and which Run 01 did
not have: the two corrections are the same operator equation with
different source functions.** MV 2020 solve a third-order ODE for `P_vv`
through the Green function of the free theory; MV 2018 solve the same
problem in the form `P_vv = g0 g0* + 2 Int S_n Im^2[g0(eta') g0*(eta)]`,
and state that form "is valid for any source function". `Im^2[...]` is the
same object as the Green function up to the Wronskian normalisation. So
the common parameterisation WP-3 needs is a **consequence** of the two
papers' own machinery, not an ansatz fitted for convenience. That is why
`maple/README.md`'s judgement that the job could not be specified was
right at the time and is now superseded.

**The free-parameter counts, computed rather than asserted.** The standing
loop forbids reporting a comparison statistic without both counts and says
no validator here will catch it. `crosscheck.py` computes them as symbolic
Jacobian ranks:

```
FREE_PARAMETERS_C = 1     (7 underlying parameters, rank 1)
FREE_PARAMETERS_D = 2     (4 underlying parameters, rank 2)
```

At fixed epoch the C side moves only in **amplitude** - its spectral index
is a pure number carrying no parameter dependence, and `rc` drops out of
the amplitude once the mode has crossed it. The D side moves in
**amplitude and shape**, because its index `p + 2n - 7` depends on the
interaction order `n` and the coupling's scale-factor power `p`. So a
C-versus-D comparison is 1 parameter against 2 and is **not** a
like-for-like comparison of two one-parameter models. Any statistic WP-3
reports must carry the counts 1 and 2.

**A shape degeneracy at a regime boundary.** Solving `m_C = m_D` gives
`p = 6 - 2n` for the inflation branch - exactly the boundary between MV
2018's own first regime (`p < 6-2n`, amplitude depends on the environment
correlation length) and its second (`6-2n < p < 10-2n`, amplitude
independent of it). At integer `n` that is `p = 4, 2, 0, -2` for
`n = 1..4`. **WP-3 must report that region as singular rather than quote a
statistic across it.** This was JOB.md tripwire 5, written before the
cross-check ran, and the cross-check found it.

Also confirmed: `m_D = p + 2n - 7` reduces correctly, `m_D = 0` at
`p = 7 - 2n` as published, and the CSL source is linear in `gamma`, degree
`-2` in `m0` and degree `2` in `Mp` in both epochs, by exact rational
scaling.

1. First version tried to expand the Green-function integrand symbolically

**But branch 1 does not apply at that `rc`.** The paper's own branch

orders of magnitude.

The condition has a clean physical reading, which is why it is worth
stating: a mode's physical wavelength at the end of inflation is
`e^DeltaN / H_end`, and the CSL terms switch on when that passes `rc`,
which the Gaussian `exp(-k^2 rc^2/2a^2)` in `alpha_k`, `beta_k` enforces.
So `H_end rc < e^DeltaN` says "the mode crossed `rc` before inflation
ended", and the two branches are the inflation-crossing and
radiation-crossing cases the paper names in those words.

**The branch boundary falls inside the laboratory-viable range.**
`rc* = 4.201e-8 m`, against the paper's own viable window `1e-8` to
`1e-4 m`. So **a single number quoted as "the Martin-Vennin bound" without
an `rc` is ambiguous by construction** - a property of the physics, and the
reason the paper published a figure. Not anybody's oversight.

**What this does not change.** Martin & Vennin's conclusion stands with
room to spare: `2.471e-86 s^-1` is still about 68 orders of magnitude below
the bottom of the laboratory-viable window, so the CMB-allowed and
lab-allowed regions remain disjoint. Every entry in the table is 50 to 80
orders below.

### What I concluded

**The discriminator is not a bispectrum, so `f_NL` cannot bound it.**
Martin & Vennin state that "All three-point correlation functions vanish in
both the two-mode squeezed and classical states", so the bispectrum is
identically zero on both sides and there is nothing for an `f_NL` limit to
constrain. The discriminating statistic is the **four-point** function, and
they characterise its shape as "not of the local type and, in fact,
corresponds to a scale dependent `g_NL`", adding that "it remains to
generate templates corresponding to this structure in order to determine
whether such a four-point function is already excluded or not".

**Planck's constrained trispectrum shapes are `g_NL^local`,
`g_NL^{pidot^4}`, `g_NL^{(partial pi)^4}` and `g_NL^B`. All four are
scale-independent amplitudes on fixed shapes, and the string
"scale-dependent" does not occur anywhere in §7.** So none matches.

**No published Planck non-Gaussianity constraint applies to the
quantum-versus-classical discriminator.** Not the `f_NL` bounds, for the
reason above; not the `g_NL` bounds, because they are template-specific and
the templates have the wrong shape.
