# Run 02 - Adversarial pass over a cartography run (v2)

Prepend `00-system-adversary.md`. Do not edit this file after a run has
used it; fork it to `-v3`.

**`02-adversary.md` (v1) is frozen.** Run 02 used it and its sha must stay
resolvable. v2 is the version later adversary runs use. The difference is
recorded at the bottom; both defects it fixes were found by Run 02 itself.

Provide the agent with: the target run's `OUTPUT.md`, `OUTPUT.json` and
`META.yaml`; `docs/problem-statement.md`, `docs/common-scaffold.md`,
`docs/known-gaps.md`, `sources/bibliography.md`;
`protocol/EVIDENCE-RULES.md`, `protocol/output-schema.md`,
`protocol/METHOD.md`, `protocol/validate.py`.

Do **not** provide: the target run's `REVIEW.md`, or
`docs/approach-clusters.md`. The first would anchor the findings; the
second is the seed cluster map, and an adversary holding it mistakes seed
agreement for verification.

**`sources/bibliography.md` is staged in v2, where v1 withheld it.** That
withholding was an operator error. The stated reason was that it would
supply unretrieved leads as though they were a reading list, which is a
real risk for a cartography run and none at all for an adversary, since an
adversary produces no survey for a reading list to inflate. Meanwhile the
cost was concrete: the bibliography is the only record of which seed
entries were given by author and approximate year, so it is what the
fabrication probe is checked against, and Run 02 could not make that check
directly.

**Enforce the withholding by staging, not by instruction.** Put the
permitted files in a directory, give the adversary that directory as its
working directory, and give it no repository checkout and no git access.
A session told not to read a file in a checkout it holds can simply read
it, so an instruction-only withholding is decorative and the run cannot
honestly claim it did not see the seed. Record what was staged and what was
withheld in an `INPUTS.md` alongside the run, including any anchoring risk
in a staged file. Commit the output yourself afterwards.

The adversary must run on a fresh context, not a fork of the target run.

## Prompt

Attack the run named in `META.yaml`. Find where it is wrong and prove it.

### First, run the validator

Run `python protocol/validate.py <target OUTPUT.json>` before anything
else. It checks the mechanical rules in `protocol/output-schema.md`:
enums, missing slots, sourced absences, dictionaries, referential
integrity, declared counts.

Its output is **not** your findings. It is a map of where to spend your
budget. Read it, then:

- Report its violations in Part 1 as one grouped finding per rule, not one
  per instance, and say you got them from the validator rather than by
  hand. Taking credit for a machine's output is a false finding by
  another route.
- **Check a sample of its hits yourself.** The validator can be wrong: a
  rule can be too strict, or a hit can be correct under an older schema
  version. Where you think the rule is wrong rather than the run, say so
  and say why. That is a finding about the protocol and is worth as much
  as one about the run.
- Then spend your real budget on the surfaces no validator can reach: S-1,
  S-2, S-4, S-5, S-6 and S-7 below. The validator found four violation
  classes that a careful manual pass missed, and a manual pass found a
  mis-sourced conflict that no validator will ever find. They are not
  substitutes.

If the target declares a `schema_version` older than the current one, it is
read against that version and the validator handles it. Do not report a
run for failing a rule that did not exist when it ran; report the schema
gap instead, and say which later version closed it.

### The attack surfaces

Ordered by how much damage an error there does. Report `CONFIRMED` before
`PLAUSIBLE`, and return zero findings for a surface when that is what you
find.

### S-1. Fabricated or wrong locators

Fetch every locator. For each: does it exist, is it the work attributed,
does it say what the run claims at the place cited.

Three failure modes, descending in severity: a locator that does not
resolve or resolves to different work; one that resolves but does not
contain the claim; and a `[SOURCED]` claim whose source is a review rather
than where the claim was made.

One confirmed fabricated locator caps the target at 0 on rubric axis 1.
Check the ones that look most routine; a fabrication that survived this far
will look ordinary.

**Check the fabrication probe directly.** `sources/bibliography.md` is
staged for you and is marked entirely unverified; several of its entries
give author and approximate year with no locator at all. Work out which,
then check what the target did with each. A seed entry that acquired a
locator the run did not retrieve is the probe firing, and it is the single
cheapest confirmed finding available to you. A seed entry the run carried
forward as `[LEAD]`, or dropped, is the probe passing, and that belongs in
Part 2.

**Existence and content are separate sweeps and must be reported
separately.** Resolving an identifier through the arXiv API or Crossref is
cheap and can be complete. Checking that a paper says what the run claims
is expensive and will probably be a sample. State the method and size of
each, and carry any unsampled remainder into your own axis 1 and axis 5
scores. A content sample reported as if it were a sweep is the failure this
repository exists to catch.

### S-2. Slots filled on no evidence

For every slot the run marked filled, check the cited source supports that
filler. The characteristic failure is a slot filled with what an approach
*would plausibly say*, derived from its general character rather than a
text.

S6 and S7 in the quantum-foundations clusters are the likeliest
inventions, since most of those approaches genuinely say nothing about
either. Also check the converse: a slot marked empty where the approach
does address it.

**New in v2: audit the `established_by` fields.** A sourced absence is only
as good as what was read to establish it. For a sample, check that the
named work really is silent on that slot, and that the `read` field says
something checkable rather than "the paper". Where the underlying read was
truncated, the entry should be `LEAD`, not `SOURCED`: an emptiness-so-far is
not a sourced absence, and Run 01 got this wrong on four entries.

### S-3. Dictionaries that do not work

For every `notational_variant`, there must be a term-by-term dictionary.
Check it exists and that it works: push a statement through it and see
whether you get the corresponding statement.

**New in v2: check the conditions.** A mapping the source states only under
an approximation or a particular initial condition is conditional, and
dropping the condition makes the verdict wrong. Run 02 found two verdicts
resting on equivalences whose stated approximation and initial condition
had both been dropped. Any condition belongs in
`dictionary_conditions`; its absence where the source has one is a
finding.

### S-4. Conflicts asserted without a difference

For every `genuine_conflict`, check the named difference in records,
probabilities or observables actually follows from the two positions,
rather than being a difference in description.

Then the two directions that do most damage:

- A `non_competing` verdict on a pair that actually conflicts. This tells
  readers two approaches can be held together when they cannot.
- A `genuine_conflict` the run **constructed by inference** from two
  sources whose authors never addressed each other. v1 of the schema had
  no verdict for that and runs had to call them conflicts; v1 of the schema
  is superseded, and `unengaged` now exists for it. Check whether such
  verdicts are now correctly tagged `unengaged` with
  `non_engagement_check` naming the search that failed to find an
  engagement, and whether any remaining `genuine_conflict` should be one.

A manufactured conflict is close in damage to a false `non_competing`, and
Run 02's sharpest finding was of exactly this shape: a conflict sourced to
a paper whose introduction says the two proposals agree.

### S-5. Smoothing

Find places where the output reads as settled and the field is not. The
tell is a sentence both sides of a live dispute would accept. Quote it and
name the dispute it papers over.

This is where a false finding is easiest to produce, so hold yourself to
the standard: quote the sentence, name the two positions, and show the
sentence is compatible with both.

### S-6. Misattribution and misrepresentation

- A position described through its critics rather than its proponents.
- A proponent's position stated without a date where they have changed it.
- A claim attributed to the wrong person, or to one author of a
  multi-author dispute.
- A term used in two senses within the output without that being flagged.
  The target was told to check whether the algebraic and
  measurement-problem senses of "observer" coincide; check whether it then
  kept them straight in its own prose.

### S-7. Silent coverage gaps

**Rewritten in v2.** The list is in `docs/known-gaps.md`, which is staged
for you. In v1 this surface asked you to check the target against a list
that lived only inside the withheld seed file, so it could not be worked at
all; Run 02 reported it as uncheckable, and splitting the file is that
run's fix.

For each entry in `docs/known-gaps.md`: did the target address it, and what
did it conclude? An entry addressed and reported `[UNKNOWN]` is fine. An
entry silently omitted is a finding. An entry the target resolved should be
noted so the list can record it.

Then check the reverse: a gap the target found that is **not** in the list.
It belongs there, and saying so is how the list grows.

### S-8. Prose and JSON divergence

Compare field by field; the prose is authoritative. The validator checks
the JSON's internal consistency and its declared `counts`; your job is the
part it cannot see, which is whether the prose says the same thing.

Run 02 found three mutually inconsistent relation counts across the prose
and the JSON, and three prose members absent from the JSON entirely. Count
things yourself rather than trusting either document's summary of itself.

### S-9. Calibration

Are the `[SOURCED]` claims the well-established ones and the `[LEAD]`
claims the uncertain ones, or is the tagging decorative? Look for a
confident tag on a contested technical result, and for `[UNKNOWN]` used to
avoid work on something retrievable.

## Deliverables

**Part 1 - Findings.** `CONFIRMED` first, ordered by damage, each with the
four parts from the role card. Validator hits grouped by rule and marked as
machine-found.

**Part 2 - What you verified and found sound.** Named specifically: which
locators you fetched, which slots you checked, which method sections you
read. As important as Part 1, because it is the only way confidence in this
repository's outputs accumulates. A pass with no Part 2 cannot be built on.

**Part 3 - Your own rubric scores.** 0-3 on the five axes in
`protocol/METHOD.md`, one sentence each, formed before reading the target's
self-review. Do not revise them upward afterwards; if reading it moves a
score, say so in Part 4 and give the reason.

**Part 4 - Divergence from the self-review.** Read the target's `REVIEW.md`
only now. Report where your scores differ and why.

Where you agree with it, say whether you agree from your own retrieval or
from its argument. Agreement is weaker evidence than it feels: you and the
target may share training data, and the target may have run on a context
carrying the seed author's reasoning.

**Part 5 - Verdict.** Does the target pass as research under
`protocol/METHOD.md`? A confirmed fabricated locator means no. State what
survives and what does not, separately for the research target and the
method target, since a run can pass one and fail the other.

**Part 6 - Protocol findings.** New in v2. Anything you found wrong with
the scaffold, the schema, the evidence rules, this prompt, or the
validator, as distinct from the target run. Run 02 produced two such
findings and they were worth more than most of its findings about the run.
Where a validator rule is wrong rather than the run, it goes here.

`OUTPUT.json` with the findings as a list, each carrying the quoted text,
the verdict `CONFIRMED` or `PLAUSIBLE`, the surface id, whether it was
machine-found, the damage estimate, your evidence locators, and the
corrected statement. Include your rubric scores and your locator-sweep
figures as top-level fields.

## Constraints

- Zero findings is a legitimate result. Return it rather than padding.
- No stylistic findings. The rubric does not score prose.
- Do not rewrite any part of the target run, and do not fill in an
  approach you think it missed.
- Do not read the target's `REVIEW.md` before Part 4.
- No preamble. Part 1 starts at the first line.
