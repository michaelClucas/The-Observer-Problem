## Prompt

Build a map of the existing approaches to the cosmological-scale observer
problem, stated in one shared notation, and report where they actually
disagree.

Read `docs/problem-statement.md` for the five sub-problems P1-P5 and
`docs/common-scaffold.md` for the seven slots S1-S7. `[SEED-ONLY]`
`docs/approach-clusters.md` is a hand-written starting partition: treat it
as a set of leads to verify, correct, and extend, not as ground truth. It
was written quickly and is wrong in places.

### What to produce

**Part 1 - Inventory.** Every distinct approach you can establish from
primary sources. For each one:

- A label, and the people whose work defines it, with a source for each
  attribution.
- Which of P1-P5 it is actually addressing. Most approaches address one or
  two and are silent on the rest. Silence is the normal case; record it.
- Each of S1-S7, filled, marked `empty`, or marked `misfit` with an
  explanation. Confidence tag and sources on every slot.

Aim for coverage over polish. If you can establish that an approach exists
but can only fill two slots, include it with five empty slots. That is more
useful than leaving it out.

**Part 2 - Relations.** For the pairs the field treats as rivals, plus any
pair you find unexpectedly related, give one of:

- `notational_variant` - and exhibit the dictionary, term by term. If you
  cannot write the dictionary, the verdict is `unresolved`, not
  `notational_variant`.
- `genuine_conflict` - and name what differs: an allowed record, a
  probability, or a predicted observable. Vocabulary difference is not
  conflict.
- `non_competing` - they fill disjoint slots or address disjoint
  sub-problems. Expect many of these. A large number of `non_competing`
  verdicts would itself be the main result of this run, because it would
  mean the field's apparent disagreement is largely people answering
  different questions.
- `unresolved` - you could not establish which. Say what is missing.

**Part 3 - Assumptions.** The load-bearing assumptions, each with who holds
it, who denies it, and which sub-problem it is load-bearing for. Separate
the pairs that are genuinely inconsistent from the pairs that merely sound
incompatible. At minimum, address:

- Whether a global quantum state of the universe exists as a physical
  object, or is a category error.
- Whether observers can be counted, and whether instance-counting is even
  the right operation.
- Whether typicality is an assumption requiring justification or a
  background condition of any prediction at all.
- Whether "observer" in the algebraic de Sitter literature denotes the
  same thing as "observer" in the measurement-problem literature. Settle
  this if you can, and if you cannot, say precisely what is unclear. This
  is the highest-priority question in the run.

**Part 4 - Scaffold report.** Where S1-S7 broke. Which approaches fit
badly, what the slots got wrong, and what you would change. A clean fit
across all approaches is a suspicious result; look for where you forced
something.

**Part 5 - Open questions.** Ranked. Each with why it matters and what
would settle it. Include questions that are answerable by reading a
specific paper you did not get to.

**Part 6 - Unverified leads.** Everything you believe but could not source
in this run, with what retrieval would confirm it. A long list is a good
outcome.

### Deliverables

`OUTPUT.md` with the six parts in order, and `OUTPUT.json` matching
`protocol/output-schema.md`. Where they disagree the prose wins, and the
disagreement is a review finding, so keep them consistent.

### Constraints

- Coverage over depth. Six clusters at three slots each beats one cluster
  at seven.
- Do not produce a synthesis, a preferred approach, or a proposed
  resolution. If you find yourself building one, stop and write it down in
  Part 5 as an open question instead.
- Do not report a technical result you cannot state precisely. "CLPW show
  that including an observer changes the algebra type" is only usable if
  you can say what the observer is required to have and what the resulting
  type is. Otherwise it is a lead.
- Several references in the seed are given by author and approximate year
  on purpose. Returning them as complete citations with locators you did
  not retrieve is a run-invalidating failure.
- No preamble, no closing summary. Part 1 starts at the first line.

### Where to look

Not a reading list, and not exhaustive. Primary sources in foundations of
quantum mechanics, quantum cosmology, inflationary perturbation theory,
the eternal-inflation measure literature, algebraic QFT and the recent
crossed-product work on de Sitter space, and the philosophical literature
on self-locating belief and anthropic reasoning. These five bodies of work
cite each other rarely, which is the reason this run exists. Expect to find
the same problem solved independently under different names, and expect at
least one case where the same name covers two different problems.
