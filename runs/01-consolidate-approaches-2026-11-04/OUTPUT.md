## Cluster A - No-collapse formulations of a closed universe

Seed correction, carried into this inventory: the seed treats decoherent
histories as one member. It is two. Wallace states the Gell-Mann/Hartle and
Griffiths/Omnes versions are distinct and that the former is much closer in
spirit to Everett (arXiv:0712.0149 fn. 4) `[SOURCED]`. They are split below
as A2a and A2b because their S4 differs by a rule that changes the set of
allowed records.

   a finding about the scaffold: it cannot see disagreement about
   justification.
3. **A2 must be split**, as above.
4. **S3 is a misfit for A2a's gravitational version.** Reported in Part 4.
5. **The set-selection premise shared by A2a's critics is under live
   attack.** Strasberg, Reinhard & Schindler, "First Principles Numerical
   Demonstration of Emergent Decoherent Histories", arXiv:2304.10258,
   Phys. Rev. X 14, 041027 (2024), equate the many-worlds preferred-basis
   problem with the histories set-selection problem and claim a solution
   from a minimal framework that relies on **none** of environmental
   decoherence, quantum Darwinism, Markov approximations, low-entropy
   initial states, or ensemble averages. Evidence is numerical: exact
   diagonalisation for histories of up to five times in a generic random
   matrix model, with decoherence emerging robustly for slow
   coarse-grained observables and coherent effects conjectured and
   observed to be exponentially suppressed in particle number. This bears
   directly on assumption 1 of A2a, since both Dowker & Kent and Wallace
   §3.1 build on non-uniqueness being fatal. Verified independently by the
   cartographer. `[SOURCED]`

   Correction to the retrieval record: a subagent reported this paper as
   also doubting that approximately decoherent sets can always be deformed

   by the paper's abstract page**, which does not address deformation at
   all. The deformation question therefore remains `[UNKNOWN]` and is

### E5. The rejection of typicality

This was the cluster's priority question. It resolves into a live,
well-documented dispute with both sides in print.

**Hartle & Srednicki, "Are We Typical?"**, arXiv:0704.2630, Phys. Rev. D 75,
123523 (2007), doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.75.123523. Title, author order, and
central claim verified independently by the cartographer. `[SOURCED]`

What they argue, precisely, because this is widely misreported:

- **They do not claim we are atypical.** The claim is that typicality cannot
  be used to infer anything from the data, and that **a theory is not
  incorrect merely because it predicts that we are atypical.** Verified
  directly against the abstract. `[SOURCED]`
- **The selection fallacy**, §IV: computing likelihoods as though we had
  been randomly selected by some physical process when there is no evidence
  for such a process. The fallacious step reasons that since I am human but
  might have been Jovian, each theory should be weighted by the fraction of
  intelligent beings that are human. They reject the resulting weighting
  factor. It would be correct only if an outside agency had randomly
  selected one intelligent being and revealed the result; **noticing that
  we are human is not such a selection.** Their formulation: we are not a
  disembodied entity that was randomly selected to have a particular
  physical description; we are the physical system the data describe.
  `[SOURCED]`
- **The correct likelihood marginalises over the unobserved count rather
  than weighting by it.** In their red/blue cycle model they show that the
  number of blue observers does not affect the probability of our data.
  `[SOURCED]`
- **Typicality may enter only through priors**, explicitly stated as such.
  Permitted in principle, but an unsupported personal preference unless
  declared. `[SOURCED]`
- **Named targets**: Page, Dyson-Kleban-Susskind, and Bousso-Freivogel-style
  arguments. `[SOURCED]`
- **§VI, which both sides later agree on**: in a complete quantum theory of
  the universe no separate typicality assumption would be needed, because
  probabilities for our observations would follow from dynamics, initial
  state and a quantum description of observers. `[SOURCED]`

**Srednicki & Hartle, "Science in a Very Large Universe"**, arXiv:0906.0042,
Phys. Rev. D 81, 123524 (2010), doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.81.123524. **Note the
author order reverses between the two papers.** `[SOURCED]`

The construction, which is the scaffold's S6 filler made precise:

- **The xerographic distribution.** The universe contains some number of
  copies of our data; having no information about which copy we are, we must
  assign a probability that we are each one. That set of probabilities is
  the xerographic distribution. **Typicality is the uniform choice.**
  `[SOURCED]`
- **There are two inequivalent uniform choices**: uniform over observers
  with our data, and uniform over all observing systems. They show these
  give different likelihoods. **So "assume typicality" is not even a single
  option.** Any later run writing typicality as one assumption is smoothing
  over a sourced ambiguity. `[SOURCED]`
- **A theoretical framework is the pair** of a fundamental theory and a
  xerographic distribution. **Observation tests the pair, not the theory
  alone.** `[SOURCED]`
- **First- versus third-person probabilities.** Third-person probabilities
  describe the whole universe, are computed from the theory alone, make no
  reference to us, and obey the usual quantum rules including Born's rule.
  First-person probabilities describe what our instance will observe and
  **require the xerographic distribution in addition.** `[SOURCED]`
- **Not derivable, even in principle**, §IX: the distribution cannot be
  determined from the dynamics and quantum state, because such a theory does
  not identify which copy is us. A footnote notes the mechanism is classical
  in nature - it would be equally necessary if the theory were classical.

and endorses their own §VI sentence. `[SOURCED]`

**The one point of agreement across the whole dispute.** Page,

### E6. Self-locating uncertainty in Everettian branching

### Priority question 1: what the de Sitter algebra result requires of the observer

Source: Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington & Witten, "An Algebra of
Observables for de Sitter Space", arXiv:2206.10780 (v1 22 June 2022, v5
3 July 2023), related DOI 10.1007/JHEP02(2023)082. Author list and arXiv
number both verified independently by the cartographer. The retrieval read
characters 0-200000 of 214074; the appendices were not read. `[SOURCED]`

**What the observer is.** One quantum degree of freedom. Its Hilbert space
is the square-integrable functions on the half-line, its Hamiltonian is the
position-like coordinate constrained to be non-negative, and the conjugate
variable is the time its clock tells. The constraint adds this observer
Hamiltonian to the generator of static-patch time translations. §2.5 states
the requirement minimally: an observer is any system that can tell time, and
additional commuting operators would just go along for the ride. **No
record, no apparatus, no pointer basis, no classicality, no macroscopicity.**
§3 states that no property except an energy has been attributed to it.
`[SOURCED]`

**The clock Hamiltonian must be bounded below, and that is the whole
mechanism.** The construction runs in two explicit stages in §2.4:

1. With the clock variable real-valued, the crossed product of the
   static-patch algebra by the one-parameter group generated by the
   static-patch Hamiltonian is **Type II∞**. A trace exists, but the trace
   of the identity **diverges**, because a constant wavefunction on the real
   line is not square-integrable. So it is a weight, not a state.
2. Imposing non-negativity by a projection yields **Type II₁**, with the
   trace of the identity equal to one: finite and normalised.

What breaks without the lower bound, precisely: there is no normalised
trace, hence no maximum-entropy state. Entropy still exists, but only up to
an undetermined additive constant, and the identification of empty de Sitter
as the maximum-entropy state - the paper's headline - is exactly what the
bound delivers. `[SOURCED]`

**What the bound costs, stated by the authors.** Footnote 8: a clock whose
energy is bounded below **cannot tell time perfectly**. With the bound
imposed there is no self-adjoint conjugate operator; it is only
approximately defined, on states supported away from the boundary, which
suffices for the semiclassical states used. `[SOURCED]`

**Algebra type before and after.** The sequence is **Type III → Type II∞
(unbounded clock) → Type II₁ (clock energy bounded below)**. The sharper
"before" statement is more striking than the type label: §2.4 establishes
that **the only invariant elements of the static-patch algebra under the
static-patch Hamiltonian are c-numbers**, because the modular automorphism
group acts ergodically and naive time averaging gives matrix elements that
are infinite or zero. So before the observer, the algebra of gauge-invariant
observables available in the static patch is trivial - multiples of the
identity and nothing else. Footnote 5 invokes Takesaki duality to show no
invariant operators have been missed. `[SOURCED]`

Note on precision: the retrieval reports CLPW's own §2.1 wording as "Type
III" without the subscript. The III₁ designation is standard in the
surrounding literature and is used by Witten's other papers for this
setting, but it is recorded here as CLPW's own wording rather than upgraded.
`[SOURCED]` for "Type III"; `[LEAD]` for III₁ being the intent.

**What becomes well-defined, in order:** a finite normalised trace; density
matrices as positive trace-one elements; entropy, defined only up to a
state-independent additive constant, which the abstract flags as unavoidable
for a Type II₁ algebra; a maximum-entropy state corresponding to empty de
Sitter, reproducing Gibbons-Hawking; and a semiclassical match to
generalized entropy. `[SOURCED]`

**One probability statement, and it runs the wrong way for P2.** §2.4
contains a relation between an outcome probability and an entropy change.
But it obtains the probability from the trace and then reads off the
entropy. **It presupposes the Born rule on the trace; it does not derive
one.** The authors call it a rather abstract explanation. This matters for
priority question 2. `[SOURCED]`

**Assumed versus derived.** Assumed: weak gravity with gravitons in
quadratic approximation, at leading order in Newton's constant; the
Bunch-Davies state with its thermal interpretation, so **a distinguished
state is an input to defining the algebra**; that the Timelike Tube Theorem
carries over to de Sitter, proven only for free fields in real-analytic
globally hyperbolic spacetimes; **non-negativity of the observer energy,
imposed by hand as physical input and not derived**; one observer on a
worldline bounding one static patch, with its localisation at the centre not
derived in the minimal model; a semiclassical observer wavefunction; and
hyperfiniteness, described as expected rather than proven. Derived: the
triviality of the invariant subalgebra, both type results, the trace,
density matrices, entropy, the maximum-entropy state, and the semiclassical
match. `[SOURCED]`

**Left open by the paper itself**, which bounds how strongly P5 can be
stated: §4.3 reports that the case with an observer in one static patch and
none in the complementary patch is troublesome, that they have no definitive
resolution, and offers a conjecture; §4.4 reports that the Euclidean
observer path integral produces an infinite delta-function, considers and
doubts a fix, and says it is entirely unclear in what sense the function
computed by the traditional Euclidean approach is an entropy at all.
`[SOURCED]`

**A correction to this repository's own problem statement.**
`docs/problem-statement.md` says the de Sitter operator algebra appears to
be well-defined *only* once an observer with a clock is included. Two
sourced results narrow that:

- Chen & Penington, arXiv:2406.02116, find nontrivial algebras of
  diffeomorphism-invariant observables in slow-roll inflation and in
  evaporating Schwarzschild-de Sitter **without introducing an external
  clock system**; the rolling inflaton and the evaporating black hole do the
  job. Both algebras are Type II∞ factors. `[SOURCED]` abstract
- Speranza, arXiv:2504.07630, argues the observer needed in semiclassical
  gravity can be **constructed from the quantum fields themselves**.
  `[SOURCED]` abstract

So the necessity of *adding* an observer is specific to exact de Sitter,
where an exact timelike Killing symmetry is what makes the invariant
subalgebra trivial. The defensible version of P5 is: in a spacetime with an
exact timelike Killing symmetry and a thermal distinguished state, a clock
degree of freedom with energy bounded below is what converts the Type III
algebra into a Type II₁ one with a trace. **Not** that an observer is
necessary for an algebra to exist. `[INFERENCE]`; premises as cited above.
Carried to Part 4 as a correction the repository owes its own P5.

### Priority question 2: is the algebraic observer the measurement-problem observer?

**No. It is a distinct technical notion that shares the word, and the two
fill disjoint scaffold slots.** This is settleable on sourced grounds.

The positive case:

1. CLPW's own definition is an observer as any system that can tell time,
   with no property but an energy. No apparatus, record, pointer basis,
   irreversibility or classical limit. `[SOURCED]`
2. **Witten states the contrast himself, in print.** In "Algebras, Regions,
   and Observers", arXiv:2303.02837, §1 contrasts ordinary quantum mechanics
   and ordinary quantum field theory, where an observer external to the
   system has the relevant capability, with gravity, where the observer must
   be included. §2 calls the timelike-worldline model a rather minimal model
   of what an observer is, concedes one could worry it is too crude, and
   notes a realistic observer would presumably also carry measuring
   equipment and a recording device. Measurement appears only as a remark
   about operator domains, not about collapse or definiteness. **What is
   asserted is a contrast of role - internal reference versus external
   measurer - not an identification, and not a claim to solve anything in
   quantum foundations.** `[SOURCED]`
3. Functionally, the algebraic observer supplies a frame for gravitational
   dressing and makes the modular automorphism inner so the crossed product
   carries a trace. De Vuyst, Eccles, Hoehn & Kirklin state flatly that the
   observers in their work and in the previous works **are nothing but
   quantum reference frames**. Verified independently by the cartographer.
   `[SOURCED]`
4. **The one published, term-by-term bridge from this literature to a
   foundations-adjacent formalism goes to the problem of time, not to
   measurement.** De Vuyst, Eccles, Hoehn & Kirklin, "Gravitational entropy
   is observer-dependent", arXiv:2405.00114, JHEP 2025, 146,
   doi:10.1007/JHEP07(2025)146, prove what they summarise as the slogan
   **"PW=CLPW"**: the CLPW description of physical states and observables is
   equivalent to a Page-Wootters reduction, i.e. to the perspective of an
   internal quantum reference frame. Title, authors, journal reference and
   the equivalence claim all verified independently by the cartographer.
   `[SOURCED]`

   The dictionary, as the retrieval tabulated it from the paper's equations
   (the paper gives no single table, so the tabulation is synthesis from
   sourced equations): clock and clock Hamiltonian ↔ the observer's
   modular/boost clock; the constraint that the total Hamiltonian annihilate
   physical states ↔ CLPW's constraint; group averaging ↔ CLPW's map;
   conditioning on a clock reading ↔ CLPW's reduction at zero clock time;
   reduced state ↔ the image of CLPW's map; dressed observable ↔ an element
   of the static-patch algebra dressed to the clock; the Page-Wootters
   projector, trivial for ideal clocks ↔ CLPW's energy-bound projection;
   the Page-Wootters algebra ↔ the crossed product; thermal-clock trace ↔
   the trace on the crossed product.

   **The caveat the paper itself attaches is the important part**: CLPW's
   energy lower bound corresponds to starting from **non-ideal** clocks with
   nontrivial projectors. So Type II₁ is **not** a bare notational variant
   of ideal Page-Wootters; the bound is an extra. `[SOURCED]`

5. CLPW does no measurement theory. A targeted retrieval of its §§4-5 found
   no discussion of probabilities, the Born rule, measurement outcomes or
   decoherence; the only probability statement in the paper uses the Born
   rule rather than deriving it. There is no preferred basis, no record
   rule, and no account of why any particular outcome obtains. `[SOURCED]`

**Who has engaged the question in print: almost nobody, and nobody
squarely.** Giddings, arXiv:2505.22708, J. Phys. A 58, 415401 (2025), is the
closest critical treatment of the observer concept here - he treats
observers as quantum subsystems whose own structure may limit observational
precision, notes it is unclear whether ideal observers exist in a full
mathematical theory, and argues gravitational dressing is the general
construction with the crossed product a special case - but **does not
discuss the measurement problem, collapse, or interpretations of quantum
mechanics** at all; his foundational discussion is about Einstein
separability, subsystems and locality. `[SOURCED]`

Harlow, Usatyuk & Zhao, arXiv:2501.02359, comes closest to doing
measurement-problem work: the closed-universe Hilbert space appears
one-dimensional and hard to reconcile with the richness of experience, and
the proposal is that an observer's experiences are approximately described
by a theory whose Hilbert-space dimension is roughly exponential in the
observer's degrees of freedom. That is P1 and P2 territory. But the observer
is still just a Hilbert space with some number of degrees of freedom, and
the abstract contains no decoherence, collapse or preferred-basis content.
`[SOURCED]` abstract only.

**Clean negative, stated as such.** The retrieval found **no paper or review
that explicitly connects the algebraic crossed-product observer to the
measurement problem**, and **none that explicitly disclaims the connection
in those terms**. Seven distinct extended searches were run, including
philosophy-side terms; the philosophy-of-physics searches returned nothing
on this at all. Search vocabulary not tried is listed in Part 6, so the

**The finding that cuts the other way, and it is from 1977.** Gibbons &
Hawking's founding paper on horizon observer-dependence (Phys. Rev. D 15,
2738) states that the derivation requires giving up observer-independent
definitions of particles, and suggests something like the Everett-Wheeler
interpretation may be needed, because the back reaction and the spacetime
metric could depend on the observer. **So the founding paper of this cluster
explicitly reached for the measurement problem, naming an interpretation, in
1977. The 2022-onward algebraic descendants, which cite it for the entropy
and reproduce its value as the trace normalisation, dropped the thread
entirely.** Neither CLPW, nor Witten's crossed-product papers, nor the
quantum-reference-frame papers pick it up. `[SOURCED]` for the substance;

**Settlement.**

| | measurement-problem observer | algebraic observer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | whatever makes a definite record | a quantised clock / quantum reference frame; one degree of freedom suffices |
| Required properties | enough structure for an irreversible, definite, basis-selected record | can tell time; energy bounded below if Type II₁ is wanted |
| Problem it answers | why any particular record exists, with what probability | which observables are gauge-invariant, and whether the algebra has a trace |
| Scaffold slots | S4, S5 | S2, S3 |
| Sub-problem | P2 | P5, and via Page-Wootters, P1 |

**Non-competing in the scaffold's sense: disjoint slots.** Not notational
variants - no dictionary exists, and the algebraic observer has no S4 filler
to map onto. Not in genuine conflict - no differing allowed record,
probability or predicted observable.

**What remains unclear, precisely.** `[UNKNOWN]` Type II₁ has a finite
trace, hence a canonical maximum-entropy state and a finite effective
dimension; and in the closed-universe line the observer's own entropy is
what makes prediction nontrivial at all. Whether the finite-trace structure
*constrains which records are possible* - an S4-type statement - appears not
to be addressed anywhere in print. What would settle it: a paper deriving a
preferred basis or a record rule from the Type II₁ structure, or showing
that the trace or entropy fixes the probability of a definite outcome in a
way not already assumed. CLPW's probability relation is the only thing in
the vicinity and it runs the other direction.

### G4. Semiclassical / WKB emergence of time

Kiefer & Singh, "Quantum gravitational corrections to the functional
Schrodinger equation", Phys. Rev. D 44(4), 1067 (1991),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.44.1067. `[SOURCED]`, APS page fetched. Kiefer,
"Conceptual issues in quantum cosmology", arXiv:gr-qc/9906100, Lect. Notes
Phys. 541, 158-187 (2000) `[SOURCED]` - **the best single source for this
entry**. Kiefer, arXiv:1401.3578, ISRN Math. Phys. 2013, 509316,
doi:10.1155/2013/509316 `[SOURCED]`; note the journal version predates the
arXiv posting.

Mechanism `[SOURCED]`: the wave functional is written as a purely
gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi phase times a matter state; the justification
is a scale hierarchy licensing Born-Oppenheimer techniques; time is then
defined by the classical gravitational background, giving a Schrodinger
equation for matter fields under small back-reaction. Kiefer & Singh add
that the matter corrections do not depend on the factor ordering of the
gravitational kinetic term, and that subleading terms imply
**gravitationally induced nonunitarity**.

S1 as G1 plus the WKB ansatz, **a restriction to a subclass of solutions**,
so not unique. S2 **empty**. S3 **a time parameter along a classical
trajectory in gravitational configuration space, read off the Hamilton-Jacobi
phase. Not quantised, not an operator, so the scaffold's spectrum question
does not apply.** Internal in origin but functioning as a background time at
the level where matter is described. S4 decoherence imported from Cluster A,
plus a low-entropy initial condition. S5 **Born rule on the matter state
only after the approximation holds**, with the inner products equivalent and
positive definite at one loop - **so the probability rule exists only after
the approximation, not before.** S6, S7 **empty**. `[SOURCED]`

**The live dispute over whether this works, which is the most philosophically
sharp exchange in the run.** Chua & Callender, arXiv:2303.14854, Philosophy
of Science 88(5) (2021), doi:10.1086/714870 `[SOURCED]`, argue against
semiclassical time on grounds of circularity: **observation is a temporal
process**, so appealing to observers to supply a standard of smallness for
the approximations brings time back in; the Born-Oppenheimer step is
thoroughly laden with temporal notions; and on decoherence, decoherence at
once requires time and is required for time.

**They also report a contradiction inside Kiefer** which this repository
should not smooth: that Kiefer says in one place that the semiclassical
approximation brings in a time parameter *before* decoherence, and in
another that the very concept of time makes sense only *after* decoherence
has occurred, and that both cannot hold. `[SOURCED]` for Chua & Callender's
report; **the attribution is theirs and was not verified against Kiefer's
book, so the underlying passages are `[LEAD]`.**

On the other side, Huggett & Thebault, arXiv:2310.11072 (17 October 2023),
no journal reference on the arXiv page `[SOURCED]`, conclude the model
coherently delivers three grades of temporal structure and that remaining
concerns are explanatory rather than consistency-based. **Whether this is a
direct reply to Chua & Callender is `[LEAD]`** - the arXiv page does not
mention them.

### Priority answer: is the dependency of prediction on time made explicit?

**Yes, by four identifiable parties, in different registers - and two of
them run the dependency in the opposite direction from the seed's framing.
That reversal is the finding.**

1. **Hartle states it by removing both at once.** His generalised
   sum-over-histories quantum mechanics is advertised as one that does not
   require either a preferred notion of time or a definition of
   measurement. **He treats a preferred time and a measurement primitive as
   the same pair of presuppositions**, though he does not order them.
   `[SOURCED]` arXiv:gr-qc/9304006
2. **Chua & Callender state it as a circularity, and run it the other way.**
   Observation presupposes time, **so you cannot use an observer to license
   the approximation that produces time.** `[SOURCED]`
3. **Unruh & Wald state it against Page-Wootters specifically**: the
   conditional probability interpretation requires a preferred time
   variable, and in ordinary quantum mechanics no dynamical variable
   correlates monotonically with the time parameter. **`[LEAD]`** - Phys.
   Rev. D 40, 2598 (1989); the APS landing page was not fetched and the

   paraphrase come from search digests. **Load-bearing for the question

4. **Goldstein & Teufel invert the dependency entirely.** They argue the two
   main conceptual problems of canonical quantum gravity, the problem of

   problem is prior to the problem of time** - the exact reverse of the
   seed's hypothesis. `[SOURCED]` arXiv:quant-ph/9902018. **Reported as a
   live disagreement about direction, not as agreement.**

**What nobody retrieved says** is that the problem of time is prior

### Priority answer: is semiclassical time doing the Page-Wootters job?

**Not settled by anything retrieved. Reported as unclear, with three
sourced differences and the reason no source closes it.**

1. **Exactness.** Semiclassical time is an approximation with a stated
   expansion parameter and known corrections, including gravitationally
   induced nonunitarity. Hoehn, Smith & Lock claim their relational
   construction gives correct transition probabilities **without
   approximations, ideal clocks, or ancilla systems**. If both claims hold,
   the two are not doing the same work: one is an asymptotic recovery, the
   other an exact rewriting. `[SOURCED]` both sides.
2. **What the clock is.** In G4 time is a parameter along a classical
   trajectory, **not an operator**. In the Page-Wootters line the clock's
   time observable is a **covariant POVM**, more general than a self-adjoint
   time operator. `[SOURCED]`
3. **Whether the relational route needs the semiclassical route.** If the
   Unruh-Wald objection stands, Page-Wootters is not an alternative to
   semiclassical time but a construction needing something like it as input,
   which would make the relation **dependency, not rivalry**. Hoehn, Smith &
   Lock argue the related Kuchar objections are incorrect. **Live.**
   `[SOURCED]` for the HSL side, `[LEAD]` for Unruh-Wald.

**Why no source closes it**, stated as negative results: Kiefer's review
does not mention Page-Wootters at all, checked against the full text;
Chua & Callender mention neither Page-Wootters nor conditional
probabilities; Isham's conditional-probability discussion is in the
unlocated part 2; and Adlam's abstract mentions neither WKB nor the
Wheeler-DeWitt equation. `[SOURCED]` as negatives. What would settle it:
Isham part 2 §6.2, Kuchar 2011 for his own words, or the full text of
Kiefer & Peter arXiv:2112.05788, which covers two proposed solutions to the
problem of time and is the likeliest single place where both appear side by
side.

## 3.1 Does a global quantum state of the universe exist as a physical object?

**Load-bearing for: P1.**

**Holds, as a normalisable state vector:** Everettians (A1); Sebens &
Carroll (E6). `[SOURCED]`

**Holds, as a non-normalisable solution of a constraint:** Page-Wootters and
its revivals (B1), where the global vectors explicitly do not admit a proper
normalisation and are regularised; Wheeler-DeWitt (G1), where the inner
product is the open Hilbert-space problem; the perspective-neutral quantum
reference frame branch (B4). `[SOURCED]` **This is a materially different
answer from the first, and the scaffold's S1 is right to distinguish them.**

**Holds, as a pair of Hamiltonian and initial state, with uniqueness an
input rather than a result:** Gell-Mann/Hartle decoherent histories (A2a).
`[SOURCED]`

**Denies outright:** relational quantum mechanics - there is no quantum
state of the universe, the universal wave function plays no role, and there
cannot be cosmic time. `[SOURCED]`

**Denies the *observer-independence*, not the state:** QBism. This is the
correction the run makes to the seed, which had QBism rejecting the
universal wavefunction as a category error. The sourced position is that a
pure state for the whole universe is **certainly allowed in principle** as
one agent's personal judgment about the universe minus herself, with the
agent required to be outside the *system of interest* rather than outside
the physical universe. **So the category error is an observer-independent
state of the universe, not a state of the universe as such.** `[SOURCED]`,
verified directly by the cartographer.

**Brackets the question deliberately:** Zurek, who calls the claim that the
universal state really existed unwarranted while declining to assert or deny
it. `[SOURCED]`

**Does not need the question:** Clusters C, D and F, each for a different
reason. C and D work with subsystem states on a fixed classical background.
F starts from an algebra attached to a subregion.

**Genuinely inconsistent pairs**: relational quantum mechanics against the
perspective-neutral frame branch (unjoined in print); relational quantum
mechanics against Everettianism; Zurek against A1 on whether the state's
reality is the point or a distraction. **Pairs that merely sound
incompatible**: QBism against quantum cosmology, which on the sourced record
disagree about observer-independence rather than about whether a universal
state may be written.

## 3.2 Can observers be counted, and is instance-counting the right operation?

**Load-bearing for: P3, P4.**

**Counting is legitimate and is the right operation for likelihoods:**
Garriga & Vilenkin (probability as a fraction of the reference class); Azhar,
Guth & Namjoo (universes weighted by matching observer-instant counts); Page
(via a normalization principle under which other observers' possible
observations lower the likelihood of ours); De Simone et al.; Linde;
Dyson-Kleban-Susskind as microstate counts. `[SOURCED]`

**Counting is legitimate but what gets counted is unsettled**: Bostrom
himself, whose reference-class problem rules out stones and bacteria, calls
chimpanzees and Neanderthals borderline, and names four zones of vagueness,
noting the problem is equivalent to deciding which objects get zero sampling
density. **The leading proponent of counting concedes the extension of
"observer" is undetermined.** `[SOURCED]`

**Counting as a weighting operation is denied outright:** Hartle & Srednicki
2007. The counting factor does not belong in the likelihood; the unobserved
count is **marginalised over**, not used as a weight; and in their model the
number of observers who see the other outcome does not affect the
probability of our data. `[SOURCED]`

**Counting is the wrong weighting for a different reason:** Sebens &
Carroll, for whom branch-counting is wrong and amplitude-squared is right.
`[SOURCED]`

**Counting is needed for third-person probabilities but underdetermines
first-person ones:** Srednicki & Hartle 2010. `[SOURCED]`

**Counting observers is avoidable entirely:** Bousso 2006, who substitutes
the entropy that can be produced within a local patch, explicitly in lieu of
anthropic criteria. **This is the only retrieved approach to P4 that does
not count observers, and it deserves more attention than the seed gave it.**
`[SOURCED]`

**Genuinely inconsistent**: Hartle & Srednicki 2007 against every member of
E1-E4. The inconsistency is about the *operation*, not the regularisation,
which is why it cannot be resolved by choosing a better measure.

## 3.3 Is typicality an assumption requiring justification, or a background condition of prediction?

**Load-bearing for: P3, and through it P4.**

**Four positions, not two.** Any later run writing this as a binary is
smoothing.

1. **An assumption, legitimate only as a declared prior or declared
   distribution.** Hartle & Srednicki 2007; Srednicki & Hartle 2010, whose
   §IX holds the xerographic distribution **cannot even in principle** be
   determined from the dynamics and quantum state, and which rejects the
   Copernican, anthropic and mediocrity principles *as universal laws*
   while allowing them as motivations for a choice; Azhar 2015, who shows
   that if both the theory and the typicality assumption are allowed to
   vary, **typicality does not always give the highest likelihoods.**
   `[SOURCED]`
2. **A background condition of prediction as such.** Azhar, Guth & Namjoo
   2026, for whom unrestricted distributions would make empirical science
   essentially impossible and without typicality theories could hardly be
   excluded by observation; Page, for whom existence-only likelihoods make
   observations useless for discrimination and are far too cheap for
   science; Garriga & Vilenkin; implicitly De Simone et al., whose
   Boltzmann-brain test presumes a typical observer. `[SOURCED]`
3. **A position both sides hold, and the most under-reported fact in the
   exchange.** That in a *complete* quantum cosmology no separate typicality
   assumption would be needed, because it would be implied. **Hartle &
   Srednicki's §VI and Page's endorsement are the same sentence** - Page
   quotes and agrees with it. **So the dispute is entirely about what to do
   in the absence of such a theory.** `[SOURCED]`
4. **A fourth position with no verified representative**: that there are no
   physically privileged choices of measure or observer proxy, making both
   researcher degrees of freedom and multiverse theories untestable.
   Attributed in search text to Norton but **no locator was obtained**, so
   it is recorded as a gap in the map rather than a position. `[LEAD]`

**And "typicality" is not one option even within position 1.** Srednicki &
Hartle give two inequivalent uniform distributions - uniform over observers
with our data, and uniform over all observing systems - and show they give
different likelihoods. `[SOURCED]`

**A dated development.** Hartle & Hertog 2017 treat observers as physical
quantum systems and **assume the observer is equally likely to be any
instance of the observational situation**, which is a uniform xerographic
distribution. It is not a retraction - the 2010 framework permits any choice
and insists only that it is a choice - but Hartle's own applied work adopts
the typicality choice he and Srednicki showed cannot be derived. Verified
independently by the cartographer. `[SOURCED]`

## 3.4 Does "observer" mean the same thing in the algebraic and measurement literatures?

**Load-bearing for: P5, and for whether cross-cluster comparison in this
field is sound at all. This was the run's highest-priority question.**

**Settled: no.** The algebraic observer is a quantised clock or quantum
reference frame; one degree of freedom suffices; it requires no record, no
apparatus, no pointer basis, no classicality and no macroscopicity, and its
own authors state that no property except an energy is attributed to it. The
measurement-problem observer is whatever produces a definite, basis-selected
record. They fill disjoint slots: S2 and S3 against S4 and S5. They address
disjoint sub-problems: P5 and, via Page-Wootters, P1, against P2.
`[SOURCED]`

**Witten states the contrast himself in print**, calling the
timelike-worldline model a rather minimal model of what an observer is,
conceding one could worry it is too crude, and noting a realistic observer
would presumably also carry measuring equipment and a recording device.
**What is asserted is a contrast of role, internal reference versus external
measurer - not an identification, and not a claim to solve anything in
quantum foundations.** `[SOURCED]`

**Three consequences.**

First, **the word is doing damage.** It denotes two different things even
*inside* Cluster F: a degree of freedom adjoined to produce a trace (CLPW)
versus a trajectory reconstructed from boundary data (Leutheusser & Liu).
Any comparison that treats the P5 literature as addressing P2 because both
say "observer" is unsound.

second, **the one published bridge goes to the problem of time, not to
measurement**, via "PW=CLPW". So the algebraic observer's nearest
foundations-adjacent relative is a conditional-probability construction
responding to P1.

Third, and this is the finding the run did not expect: **the founding paper
of horizon observer-dependence reached for the measurement problem, and its
descendants dropped the thread.** Gibbons & Hawking 1977 state that the
derivation requires giving up observer-independent definitions of particles
and suggest something like the Everett-Wheeler interpretation may be needed,
because the back reaction and the spacetime metric could depend on the
observer. The 2022-onward algebraic literature cites that paper for the
entropy and reproduces its value as the trace normalisation, and **neither
CLPW, nor Witten's crossed-product papers, nor the quantum-reference-frame
papers pick up the interpretive thread.** `[SOURCED]` for the substance; the
exact 1977 wording is `[LEAD]` and should be quoted from the original before
anyone builds on it.

**A clean negative, bounded.** No paper or review was found that explicitly
connects the algebraic crossed-product observer to the measurement problem,
and none that explicitly disclaims the connection in those terms. Seven
extended searches, including philosophy-side terms, returned nothing on
this. Search vocabulary not tried is listed in Part 6, so the negative is
bounded rather than absolute. `[SOURCED]` as a negative result.

## 3.5 Is quantum theory exact, or approximately valid?

**Load-bearing for: P2. Not on the prompt's list, and it is the cleanest
flat contradiction in the inventory, so it is recorded here.**

**Approximately valid, and false as stated:** the entire collapse programme.
The collapse term is a modification of the dynamics, not an interpretation
of it. `[SOURCED]`

**Exact:** every member of Clusters A, B, D, F and G. Cluster D in
particular keeps unitary dynamics exactly and attributes classicality to
squeezing, environment tracing or purity loss. `[SOURCED]`

These cannot both hold, and unusually for this field **the disagreement is
empirically live**: the two programmes modify the same observable, the
curvature power spectrum, with different functional forms. See Part 2 and
the open question in Part 5.

## 3.6 The clock's energy spectrum

**Load-bearing for: P1 and P5 simultaneously. This is the run's most
unexpected structural finding and it was not anticipated by the seed.**

The same spectral property is the pivot in three separate clusters, with
**different and partly opposite requirements**:

- **Cluster G (Wheeler-DeWitt).** The relevant kinetic term is **not
  bounded below** - the scale factor enters with opposite sign to the matter
  and anisotropy directions - and that unboundedness is precisely why the
  Klein-Gordon-type inner product fails to be positive definite. The
  unboundedness is a *problem*. `[SOURCED]`
- **Cluster B (Page-Wootters).** The GLM revival **requires** an unbounded
  clock generator; they state plainly that an unbounded Hamiltonian must be
  used, and the global state is non-normalisable in consequence. Hoehn,
  Smith & Lock's covariant-POVM generalisation is what buys bounded and
  half-bounded spectra, **at the price of non-orthogonal, imperfectly
  distinguishable clock readings**; and Pauli's objection is tied
  specifically to the spectrum being unbounded below. The unboundedness is a
  *requirement* in the original and a *removable restriction* in the
  generalisation. `[SOURCED]`
- **Cluster F (de Sitter crossed product).** The clock Hamiltonian **must be
  bounded below**, and that is the entire mechanism: unbounded gives Type
  II∞ with a divergent trace of the identity and entropy defined only up to
  an undetermined constant, while bounded below gives Type II₁ with a finite
  normalised trace, density matrices, and a maximum-entropy state
  identified with empty de Sitter. CLPW's own footnote concedes the cost: a
  clock whose energy is bounded below **cannot tell time perfectly.** The
  boundedness is a *requirement*, in the opposite direction from B.
  `[SOURCED]`

**And the three are connected by a published equivalence.** Since
"PW=CLPW" holds, and since the same paper notes that CLPW's energy lower
bound corresponds to **non-ideal clocks with nontrivial projectors**, the
B-versus-F tension is not a contradiction but a trade-off along one axis:
**a clock that tells time perfectly has an unbounded spectrum and gives no
finite trace; a clock with a finite trace cannot tell time perfectly.** Both
communities have independently found the same trade-off and named its two
ends differently. `[INFERENCE]`; premises: the GLM unboundedness requirement,
the Hoehn-Smith-Lock POVM result, CLPW §2.4 and footnote 8, and the De Vuyst
et al. non-ideal-clock caveat, each `[SOURCED]`.

**Why this matters beyond bookkeeping.** A bounded-below clock Hamiltonian
is what one expects physically, since energies are bounded below; an
unbounded one is what the Wheeler-DeWitt kinetic term actually has. So the
trade-off sits exactly on the fault line between what quantum cosmology's
formalism supplies and what a physical observer can be. **No retrieved
source states the trade-off in these terms**, which makes it the run's
leading candidate for a genuine synthesis - and therefore, per this run's
constraints, an open question in Part 5 rather than a conclusion here.

# Part 4 - Scaffold report

The scaffold is `docs/common-scaffold.md` v0. It did useful work: the
slot-complementarity result in Part 2 is visible only because the slots
exist. It also broke in seven identifiable ways, listed worst first. A clean
fit would have been a suspicious result; this is not that.

## 4.1 S1 conflates two things and must be split

**The most serious break.** S1 asks for "the fundamental mathematical object
describing the universe as a whole" and offers, among its fillers, "an
algebra of observables with no preferred state". **No approach in Cluster F
can use that filler**, because every crossed-product construction is a
crossed product **by the modular automorphism group of a specific state** -
Bunch-Davies in de Sitter, the thermofield double in AdS, or a conjectural
state with geometric modular flow. **The algebra cannot be specified without
first specifying a state.** `[SOURCED]`

S1 also fails for F in three further ways: it presumes a *global* object,
whereas these constructions attach to a proper subregion with a
complementary algebra; the *type* of the object changes depending on whether
the observer is included, so S1 cannot be filled once; and the only
retrieved attempt at a genuinely global algebraic S1 uses the no-boundary
state as a universal maximum-entropy reference, which imports Cluster G.

**Recommended fix:** split S1 into **S1a, the algebra or state space**, and
**S1b, the distinguished state its definition requires** (which may be
"none"). This would also sharpen Cluster B, where the thermal time
hypothesis is the one approach whose S1 genuinely *is* an algebra with no
preferred state - a distinction the current scaffold cannot express.

## 4.2 S1 is a misfit for two entire clusters

Clusters C and D work with a **subsystem** state on a fixed classical
background. Neither supplies a global object at all, and for C this is
structural rather than incidental, since the formalism is non-relativistic
in origin. Filling S1 for them at all requires reading the slot as "the
object the approach actually works with", which is not what it asks.

## 4.3 S3 presupposes that a clock exists, and its absence is often the content

S3 asks what plays the role of time "and is it internal or external", then
instructs the run to record the clock's spectrum. **For five entries the
presupposition fails**, in different ways:

- **Wheeler-DeWitt (G1):** there is no clock, the operator is a constraint
  rather than a generator of evolution, and **the absence is the entire
  content of the approach.** `[SOURCED]`
- **Thermal time (B5):** the filler is the **modular flow of the state**,
  which is not a Hamiltonian clock, so "what is its spectrum" has no
  referent as posed. `[SOURCED]`
- **Bostrom and Hartle-Srednicki (E):** the index is a reference-class
  membership or a spacetime location label, and the distribution over it is
  classical - a footnote in the latter notes the mechanism would be needed
  even in a classical theory. The spectrum question has no referent.
  `[SOURCED]`
- **Semiclassical time (G4):** the filler is a parameter along a classical
  trajectory, not an operator, so again no spectrum.
- **Gell-Mann/Hartle's gravitational version (A2a):** a four-dimensional
  spacetime coarse-graining replaces the clock entirely; the honest entry is
  "no clock; spacetime coarse-graining instead".

**Recommended fix:** make S3's first question "is there a clock at all?",
with "no, and here is what replaces it" a first-class filler rather than a
misfit. Keep the spectrum question as a sub-question that applies only when
the answer is yes - where, as Part 3.6 shows, it is extremely productive.

## 4.4 S3 is simultaneously the best and the worst slot in the scaffold

For Clusters B and F, S3 is **the primary discriminator**: the whole
difference between Type II∞ and Type II₁, and between ideal and non-ideal
Page-Wootters clocks, lives in the clock's spectrum. The scaffold's
instruction to record it is vindicated.

For Clusters C and D, **every single approach fills S3 identically** -
external background conformal time, unquantised, no clock degree of freedom
- so the slot **cannot discriminate among them at all.**

A slot that is the sharpest instrument in two clusters and a constant in two
others is not broken, but a revised scaffold should say which slots are
expected to discriminate in which regions of the problem, rather than
presenting seven slots as uniformly informative.

## 4.5 The S4/S5 separation fails for Page-Wootters

The scaffold notes that some approaches deny S4 and S5 are separable, and
Page-Wootters is one: its only candidate record rule **is** the
conditional-probability construction, so conditioning is a probability
construction rather than a definiteness mechanism, and splitting the two
produces a misfit rather than two entries. `[INFERENCE]`

## 4.6 S6 and S7 fuse in the causal-patch family, and S7 is demoted by Hartle-Srednicki

**Fusion:** for the causal patch the cutoff *is* the self-location rule. The
scaffold's separation of "how is the observer located" from "how are
infinite counts regularised" cannot be maintained there, and the entry has
to be recorded as fused. `[INFERENCE]`

**Demotion, which is a result rather than a misfit:** the scaffold treats S7
as the rule that fixes ratios of divergent counts and thereby fixes
predictions. Srednicki & Hartle accept a measure is needed for *third-person*
probabilities, accept that solving the measure problem would make a uniform
distribution well defined, and **deny that it fixes the prediction**,
because the choice of self-location rule remains and any freedom left in the
measure should be absorbed into it. So for them S7 is **neither filled nor
empty**: it is demoted from "the rule that fixes ratios" to "an input that
underdetermines the first-person probability". For their 2007 paper it is
closer to an outright misfit, since what is denied there is instance-counting
as a weighting operation at all. `[SOURCED]`

## 4.7 The scaffold cannot see disagreement about justification

Cluster A contains **three mutually independent derivations of the same S5
output**: decision-theoretic rationality, envariance, and "probabilities are
basic". The cluster agrees on the probability rule and disagrees about why.
The scaffold's comparison rule requires a differing probability, record or
observable for `genuine_conflict`, so it must classify these as
`non_competing` - which is formally correct and substantively misleading.

**Recommended fix:** add a verdict `same_output_different_grounds`, or a
per-slot field recording what argument is offered for the filler. The latter
is already half-present, since several slots ask "derived or postulated",
and extending that consistently to S5 would capture it.

## 4.8 A missing dimension: the direction of explanatory dependency

Several of the most interesting relations found are not disagreements about
slot contents but about **which problem is prior**. Chua & Callender argue
observation presupposes time, so an observer cannot license the
approximation that produces time. Goldstein & Teufel argue the problem of
time and the observable problem both come from orthodox quantum theory's
observer-dependence and dissolve given ontological clarity, making the
observer problem prior. Hartle treats a preferred time and a measurement
primitive as one pair of presuppositions. **The scaffold has no place to
record any of this**, because it assumes the sub-problems are independent
coordinates.

**Recommended fix:** a cross-cutting field per approach recording claimed
dependencies among P1-P5, with direction. On the evidence of this run that
field would be unusually well populated.

## 4.9 What the scaffold got right

Recorded because a scaffold report that only complains is not a report. S1's
distinction between a normalisable state vector and a non-normalisable
constraint solution tracked a real and consequential difference in Cluster
G and Cluster B. The instruction to record the clock's spectrum in S3
produced the run's best structural finding. The requirement to exhibit a
dictionary before claiming notational variance prevented at least three
loose equivalence claims from being recorded as variants, and the
requirement to name a differing observable before claiming conflict is what
made the slot-complementarity result visible. The instruction to record
emptiness as a result is what made Part 2.1 possible at all; a scaffold that
let empty slots be omitted would have hidden it.

# Part 5 - Open questions, ranked

Each with why it matters and what would settle it. Ranked by how much the
map changes if answered.

**1. Does the Type II₁ structure constrain which records are possible?**
Type II₁ has a finite trace, hence a canonical maximum-entropy state and a
finite effective dimension; and in the closed-universe line the observer's
own entropy is what makes prediction nontrivial at all. Whether that
finite-trace structure constrains the *set of possible records* - an S4-type
statement - appears to be addressed nowhere in print. **Why it matters:** it
is the only route found by which Cluster F could turn out to bear on P2
after all, which would overturn this run's answer to its own highest-priority
question. **What would settle it:** a derivation of a preferred basis or a
record rule from the Type II₁ structure, or a demonstration that the trace
or entropy fixes an outcome probability in a way not already assumed. CLPW's
own probability relation is the only thing in the vicinity and it runs the
other way, assuming the Born rule on the trace.

**2. Can the observer's energy lower bound be derived rather than imposed?**
CLPW impose non-negativity of the observer's energy by hand as physical
input. Since Part 3.6 shows the bound is the entire mechanism separating
Type II₁ from Type II∞, whether it is derivable decides whether the de
Sitter entropy normalisation is physics or a modelling choice. **What would
settle it:** the retrieval identified a January 2026 preprint
(arXiv:2601.07910, Klinger, Kudler-Flam & Satishchandran, *Generalized
Entropy is von Neumann Entropy II*) reported to argue that proper accounting
of the observer's rotational kinetic and binding energy implies Type II₁.
**It was not fetched. This is the single highest-value unretrieved item in
the run.** Also relevant: Espindola & Ali, who give a *different* condition,
positivity of the observer kernel, and note boundedness does not imply
positivity for the conformal sector.

**3. Is the clock trade-off in Part 3.6 a real theorem, and has anyone
stated it?** The run found that a clock telling time perfectly needs an
unbounded spectrum and yields no finite trace, while a clock with a finite
trace cannot tell time perfectly - with the two ends named independently by
the relational and algebraic communities and connected by the published
"PW=CLPW" equivalence. **No retrieved source states it in these terms.**
**Why it matters:** if it is a theorem it is a genuine constraint on what a
cosmological observer can be, and it would be the first result to bind
Clusters B, F and G together. **What would settle it:** the De Vuyst et al.
papers' later sections on degenerate and non-ideal clocks, which the
retrieval did not reach, and the Hoehn-Smith-Lock companion paper on
degenerate clock Hamiltonians.

**4. Do collapse and decoherence predictions for the curvature power
spectrum discriminate against data?** Both programmes modify the same
observable with different functional forms, and the assumption underneath
them - whether quantum theory is exact or approximately valid - is the
cleanest flat contradiction in the field. **No paper was found that sets the
two predictions against each other and asks which the data prefer.** **Why
it matters:** it is the only place in the entire map where a
genuinely-conflicting pair of approaches to P2 could be settled
observationally rather than argued. **What would settle it:** a forward
citation search for work citing both the open-EFT decoherence constraints
and the CSL cosmological exclusion.

**5. Does Page-Wootters survive the Unruh-Wald objection, and is
semiclassical time doing the same job?** If the conditional-probability
interpretation requires a preferred time variable, then Page-Wootters is not
an alternative to semiclassical time but a construction needing something
like it as input - which converts a rivalry into a dependency and relocates
Cluster B inside Cluster G. **What would settle it:** the Unruh-Wald paper
itself (currently a lead with unverified pagination), Isham's unlocated part
2 §6.2, Kuchar 2011, or Kiefer & Peter arXiv:2112.05788, which covers two
proposed solutions to the problem of time and is the likeliest single place
where both appear side by side.

**6. Is the Gell-Mann/Hartle records condition substantive or a theorem?**
Whether "strong decoherence", defined by permanence of generalized records,
is an extra constraint or follows from medium decoherence decides whether
Everettianism-plus-decoherence and decoherent histories differ in S4 at all
beyond the single-framework rule. **What would settle it:** a source stating
whether strong decoherence is generically satisfied by quasiclassical
coarse-grainings, or a counterexample. Also directly relevant: Strasberg,
Reinhard & Schindler, Phys. Rev. X 14, 041027 (2024), who claim a
set-selection solution relying on none of environmental decoherence, quantum
Darwinism, Markov approximations, low-entropy initial states or ensemble
averages - which attacks the premise both Dowker-Kent and Wallace build on.

**7. Hartle-Srednicki against Sebens-Carroll on derivability.** One holds
the self-location rule cannot even in principle be derived from the dynamics
and quantum state; the other claims to derive it from the epistemic
separability principle. **Neither engages the other anywhere retrieved.**
**Why it matters:** it is a flat contradiction about what is derivable,
between the two most careful treatments of P3 in the field, and it sits
exactly on the only bridge between the two halves of the map. **What would
settle it:** a direct comparison, which does not appear to exist; the
nearest existing input is Kent's critique of the separability principle,
for which **no locator was obtained.**

**8. Does the double-scaled SYK Type II₁ result make the clock
dispensable?** In that model the Type II₁ structure arises from the chord
algebra itself with no observer added, the observer entering only in the
bulk interpretation. If the identification with the de Sitter static patch
holds, the boundary model produces the same algebra type without a clock,
**which would make the clock a feature of the bulk presentation rather than
of the physics.** **What would settle it:** the static-patch dictionary
sections of Xu arXiv:2403.09021, not read; and the Rahman-Susskind dispute
over the Narovlansky-Verlinde proposal, neither item fetched.

**9. Is there a conceptual link between cosmic variance and the measure
problem?** Searched twice and not found; the nearest result treats cosmic
variance as **the noise floor that masks measure dependence**, which is the
opposite of identifying them. **Why it matters:** observational cosmology
has a worked, frequentist treatment of having one sky, and if it is
genuinely continuous with the measure problem then one of the field's hardest
problems has an existing partial solution in a neighbouring discipline.
**What would settle it:** a paper deriving cosmic variance as a special case
of a self-locating measure, or treating the choice of observable volume as a
measure choice; or a citation search for work citing both a measure paper
and a cosmic-variance methodology paper.

**10. Did the algebraic literature's abandonment of the Everett-Wheeler
thread leave anything behind?** Gibbons & Hawking suggested in 1977 that
observer-dependence of the metric might require something like
Everett-Wheeler, and the modern descendants cite them for the entropy while
dropping the interpretation. **What would settle whether anything was lost:**
reading the 1977 paper's own argument for the suggestion directly, which this
run did not do - it has only a database paraphrase - and checking whether any
intermediate paper engaged it.

**11. A synthesis this run declined to build.** Per the prompt's
constraints, the following is recorded as a question and not pursued. The
slot-complementarity result in Part 2.1, the clock trade-off in Part 3.6, and
the "PW=CLPW" bridge together suggest that P1 and P5 may be a single problem
in two vocabularies, with the clock's spectrum as the shared variable, while
P2 and P4 remain genuinely separate problems attacked by non-overlapping
communities. **Whether that is a real unification or an artefact of the
scaffold is exactly what a later run should test, and it should be tested by
someone who did not build the scaffold.**

**12. Is Gomez's observational prediction from the crossed-product algebra
sound?** It is the only retrieved member of Cluster F claiming a prediction
from the algebra, which makes it the only route from the algebraic programme
to item 4 of the repository's own progress list. **What would settle it:** a
reading of the derivation, and the companion paper arXiv:2304.11845, not
fetched; plus arXiv:2407.20671, reported to argue the pure de Sitter algebra
defined as a limit is algebraically nonexistent, which if right is a direct
criticism of CLPW's starting point.
