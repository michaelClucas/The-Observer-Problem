# The cosmological-scale observer problem

## Scope of this repository

Quantum theory, in every textbook formulation, assigns a special role to
measurement. A system is prepared, an observable is chosen, an outcome is
recorded. The recording apparatus sits outside the system being described.

Cosmology removes the outside. The object of study is the whole universe,
including every apparatus and every physicist. There is no external frame in
which to prepare a state, no second copy of the universe against which to
calibrate, and no ensemble of universes over which to average. Applying
quantum theory to the universe therefore requires either (a) a formulation
with no external observer, (b) an account of how an *internal* observer
acquires definite records, or (c) an argument that the question is
ill-posed.

That cluster of requirements is what this repository calls **the
cosmological-scale observer problem**. It is not one problem. It is at least
five, and they are usually attacked by different communities who cite each
other rarely.

## The five sub-problems

**P1. Closure.** How is quantum theory formulated for a system with nothing
outside it? The Wheeler-DeWitt equation has no time parameter and no
external clock, so even stating a prediction requires extra structure.

**P2. Definiteness.** Why does any particular record exist? Primordial
perturbations begin as quantum field modes in a (near) vacuum state and end
as classical temperature anisotropies in the CMB, with no measuring device
anywhere in the causal history. Something turns amplitude into record.

**P3. Localisation.** Where and when is the observer? In a universe large
enough to contain many observers with identical evidence, the physical state
plus the dynamical laws do not fix which of them you are. Predictions
therefore require a rule for self-location.

**P4. Measure.** How are observers counted? In eternally inflating or
sufficiently long-lived spacetimes, the number of observer-instances of
every type diverges. Ratios of divergent counts depend entirely on the
regularisation (the "measure"), and different measures give different
physics.

**P5. Observer-dependence of the observables.** What counts as an observable
depends on who is asking. Horizon thermodynamics, the Unruh effect, and the
de Sitter static patch all make particle content, entropy, and even the
algebra of observables frame-relative. Recent algebraic work goes further:
in de Sitter space the operator algebra appears to be well-defined *only*
once an observer with a clock is included in the description. On that
reading the observer is not an interpretive add-on; it is part of the
kinematics.

## What would count as progress

The experiment does not assume the problem is solvable. It assumes the
current state of the literature is *unmapped*, and that mapping it is a
prerequisite for anything else. Specifically:

1. A single notation in which the main approaches can be stated side by
   side, so that two approaches can be checked for genuine disagreement
   rather than vocabulary difference.
2. An explicit list of which sub-problem each approach addresses and which
   it leaves untouched. Most approaches silently solve one and assume
   another away.
3. A list of load-bearing assumptions, with the pairs that are actually
   inconsistent separated from the pairs that merely sound incompatible.
4. A short list of places where the approaches make different predictions,
   formal or observational.

Items 1-3 are the target of Run 01. Item 4 is deferred.

## Explicit non-goals

- Producing a new interpretation of quantum mechanics.
- Adjudicating which approach is correct.
- Treating any approach as refuted because it is unpopular, or as
  established because it is popular.
- Generating prose that reads as authoritative without traceable sources.
